# SCHABACKER_THEORY_1930: Stock Market Theory and Practice (R. W. Schabacker, 1930)
Book file: books/public-domain/30_Schabacker_Stock-Market-Theory-and-Practice_1930.pdf  |  Note: books/notes/30_Schabacker_Stock-Market-Theory-and-Practice.md
Status: independent book strategy; not part of Abhi's method; reference/testing only.

Page refs are PDF pages (Ch XX-XXV: printed page N = p.(N+62); see the note for the other offsets). Wording is a paraphrase, not a quotation, unless marked. The method lives in Ch XXI-XXV (p.573-719), applied to the averages in Ch XXVI-XXVII (p.741-771).

## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
Schabacker reads a daily high-low-close bar chart with volume (p.655-656) to find where accumulation or distribution is being completed, which he links mostly to pool operations (p.662-663). Seven formations mark a coming major move: head and shoulders, common turn, triangle (coil), ascending bottom / descending top, double bottom/top, complex (double head and shoulders) and broadening formation (p.663-683). Inside a running move, symmetrical, ascending and descending triangles and the ascending peak / descending bottom signal continuation (p.688-697). The signal to act is the break: in most cases the first break from the narrowing range decides the direction (p.668); the break-away gap at the end of a formation is "the final signal to get aboard" (p.714-715); and support or resistance broken by a material margin means the move runs on in the direction of the break, which he trades with a stop that reverses the position (p.702-706). Volume separates real moves from shake-outs (p.707-708), and growing irregularity on high volume after a straight run warns of distribution (p.699-701).

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** major movement vs intermediate movement. A normal intermediate reaction retraces one-third to one-half of the prior major move; less is strong, more than one-half suggests reversal (p.624-626). Technical trend = parallel lines through two or more terminal points; a close outside the channel suggests a turn; rallies failing to reach the upper line show weakness (p.709-712). Trades with the trend line's direction are more dependable than against it (p.710).
- **Levels:** support and resistance are narrow zones (one to ten points) where price repeatedly stops; more touches and longer formation make them stronger; usually they hold, and a material break is a strong signal (p.593, p.702-705, p.756). Overhead supply from earlier congestion explains resistance (p.627-629).
- **Formations:** seven reversal formations (p.663-683); continuation triangles and ascending peak / descending bottom (p.688-697); intermediate reversals are small, short editions of the seven (p.698).
- **Volume:** declines through a triangle (p.668, p.700); jumps on the break-out (p.685, p.700); high on shake-outs, which close back near prior levels (p.707-708); stays high while the bars turn erratic at distribution (p.699-701); shrinks at support after a shake-out in a bull move ("never sell a quiet market short") (p.591-594).
- **Bar:** reversal on comparatively large volume marks the end of a move (p.630); a day holding most of its gain at the close, without convulsive range or extreme volume, favours continuation (p.629-630).
- **Gaps:** common (covered), break-away (not covered, begins the move), exhaustion (after a long fast run, covered soon, marks the end) (p.713-718).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown; the book states each mirror for the short side (p.663-664, p.692-697, p.704-705).
- **S1 - Major move identified:** the chart shows a prior major movement (long run of rising swings, or of falling swings for the short side), so a formation can be read as reversal (after the move) or continuation (inside it) (p.663-664, p.690, p.698).
- **S2 - Reversal formation completed:** one of the seven formations is visible at the end of a decline: head and shoulders bottom, rounded upward turn, triangular bottom, ascending bottom (flat side with each low higher), double/triple bottom, complex bottom, or broadening bottom (five swings each wider than the last, two tops and three bottoms) (p.663-683).
- **S3 - Triangle / coil:** price range narrows bar by bar between a falling line of highs and a rising line of lows toward an apex (p.666-668, p.684, p.690).
- **S4 - Volume dries up in the formation:** volume bars decline while the triangle or base forms (p.668, p.700).
- **S5 - Continuation triangle in an advance:** inside a running advance, a symmetrical triangle, or an ascending triangle (flat line of highs with rising lows), forms after the move (p.690-696).
- **S6 - Support zone:** two or more lows stop in one narrow horizontal zone; each later test holds there (p.593, p.702-704).
- **S7 - Resistance zone above:** two or more highs stop in one narrow horizontal zone above price (p.704-705).
- **S8 - Normal reaction:** the latest pullback retraced no more than about one-half (better under one-third) of the prior advance (p.624-626).
- **S9 - Quiet at support after a shake-out:** after a sharp drop in an advance, price hovers near support while volume shrinks (p.591-594).
- **S10 - Shake-out read:** a sharp, short move against the expected direction near the apex or support, on high volume, with closes back near earlier levels (a false move, not a reversal) (p.706-709).
- **S11 - Ascending peak:** after a wide one-bar run-up, a short series of bars each with a higher high and a higher low (p.696-697).
- **S12 - Trend channel intact:** price holds within parallel lines through the swing lows and swing highs of the advance, pulling back to the lower line (p.709-712).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
- **A. Break from the formation.** In most cases the direction in which price first breaks from the narrowing range or pivot decides the major direction (p.668); the first large move away from a narrowing trading range shows the direction, barring false moves (p.644). Miami Copper "broke out" of its long triangle on the up side with a jump in volume: "a buy" (p.685).
- **B. Break-away gap.** A gap at the exit from the formation that is not covered confirms the analysis and is "the final signal to get aboard" (p.714-715).
- **C. Support / resistance broken by a material margin.** A break of a support or resistance zone by a material margin signals that the move will run further and faster in the direction of the break; resistance broken = buy, support broken = sell (p.702-705). He executes it with orders resting beyond the level (p.548-549, p.706).
- **D. Trading at the level with a reverse stop.** Buy near a support zone with an order below it to sell twice the holding (sell short near resistance with an order above it to buy twice), so a break flips the position (p.703-706); also used at support with a coupled order (p.604-605).
- **E. Trend-line buy.** In an up channel, buy at the lower trend line (p.710-712).
- For direction while a triangle forms, the book also wants fundamentals (p.668, p.685); that part is not coded (section 7).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- A support zone is broken by a material margin (long read wrong; the reverse stop switches to short) (p.703-704).
- A resistance zone is broken by a material margin (short read wrong; switches to long) (p.704-706).
- The move against the position continues without a volume increase: more likely a real reversal than a false move (p.707-708).
- A reaction goes beyond one-half of the prior major move: the major trend may be reversing (p.626).
- Price closes through the trend line opposite to the position (lower line for a long) (p.710-712).
- Rallies fail to reach the upper trend line, with poorer action at each high (weakness developing) (p.712).
- Growing irregularity on high volume after a straight advance (distribution; at least a sharp intermediate reaction) (p.699-701).
- A broadening formation makes a sixth reversal beyond the prior extreme: the formation is spoiled (p.680-682, p.749).
- For a short taken on a top formation: the averages go through old highs into new high ground; cover and reverse (p.746, p.750).
- A secondary reaction that goes below the low of the primary reaction weakens the case for renewal of the advance (p.651).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Take losses, let profits run; protect every purchase with a stop-loss, generally 3-6 points below cost, set after studying the stock's normal action (p.598-599).
- Stops at least a point or more beyond support or resistance, far enough away to avoid shake-outs (p.601, p.707).
- Reverse ("double", "switching") stop: an order for twice the position beyond the level, so a break flips the position; examples: buy around 160, 158 and 157 above a 153-158 support with the reverse stop a little under that zone; buy near 70 with the reverse stop at 68; short near 72-73 with the reverse stop at 74 (p.558-559, p.703-705).
- Always use an actual or mental stop on short sales; an actual stop is safer (p.558, p.560, p.772).
- Profit-or-loss formula: decide in advance, e.g. a 5-point loss or 15-point gain, or 3 and 10; a 3-point loss against a 5-point profit is about the limit; at support, buy 76 with a coupled order "sell at 86 or 72 stop" (p.602, p.604-605).
- Never accept a smaller profit than the possible loss (p.606).
- Progressive stop: bought at 100 with a stop at 95; at 110 move to 108, at 120 to 117, at 130 to about 126; the author prefers this to the fixed formula (p.602-604).
- Right three times out of five with small losses gives steady profit (p.600, p.605).
- Pyramiding only with experience and without trading "up to the hilt"; averaging down is illogical (p.561-566).
- Long-swing campaigns: buy on a scale down, sell on a scale up, about 10% at a time every few points, never completing the whole line at once (p.771-772).
- Paper trade honestly for two to three months; start real trading small; keep paper lots the size of real lots (p.607-610).
- Cut risk to where it "doesn't matter much"; take a trader's vacation after anxiety or a losing run; set no fixed money goal; beware over-confidence (p.612-619).
- On the exhaustion gap, switch to the opposite side (p.714-715).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- "Time element": the longer a triangle or formation takes, the larger the next move (p.684, p.688, p.696, p.744, p.756).
- Precedent by time: expecting a new triangle to last as long as the earlier ones (Miami Copper) (p.685-686).
- Support/resistance formation time "from one day to a year or more" as a strength measure (p.593).
- Bar-count limits: ascending peak "rarely more than three to five days"; shake-out over "in a couple of days, or a couple of hours"; secondary reaction timing (p.651-652, p.696, p.708).
- Window dressing before holidays and week-end profit-taking (p.586-588).
- Closing-price momentum carried into the next day's session (p.588-590).
- Hourly "tides" of the trading day (sell at opening, about 1 p.m. and the close; buy about 11 and 2) (p.590).
- End of a reaction shown by a morning dip and afternoon recovery (or the mirror) (p.592-593).
- Odd-lot and opening-order rules; wide openings after overnight news; buying the opening after bad overnight news (p.550-555, p.580-582, p.590-591).
- Business, credit and stock cycles of 3-6 years (five-year cycle) (p.722-725).
- Interest-rate lag of 3-18 months (about six months normal); bond prices turning 6-8 months before stocks (p.739-740, p.760).
- Sloan's five long-pull / long-swing tests (p.726-729).
- Fundamentals: earnings, P/E "ten times earnings", dividends, business indexes, call money, time money, brokers' loans and loan ratio, rediscount rate, public savings (p.516-543, p.734-740, p.759-760).
- Pool and banking-support detection by capitalization, floating supply, insiders and publicity stages (p.630-648, p.662-663).
- News and sentiment: selling when good news is out, following or crossing the crowd, tips, "New Era" talk, end-of-bull and end-of-bear public mood (p.577-582, p.759-767).
- Group leaders and laggards (needs other charts and group data) (p.578-579).
- Fundamentals deciding a triangle's direction (p.668, p.685, p.688).
- Options, arbitrage and selling against the box (p.566-572, p.594-597, p.772-773).

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- "Material margin", "high volume", "narrow", "sharp" have no numeric thresholds; the only figures are stops "at least a point or more" beyond a level (p.707) and support zones "one to 10 points" wide (p.593), both 1920s stock points with no scaling rule. Not defaults.
- The book admits formations are "very seldom" perfect and need imagination (p.690), says trend lines are over-emphasised (p.709, p.713), and that charts must not be used alone (p.655, p.662).
- While a triangle forms the book takes the direction from fundamentals (p.668); a chart-only read can only wait for the break (trigger A). Conditions S2-S5 can therefore be met without a direction until the trigger.
- Shake-out vs real break is judged by volume and closes (S10, invalidation 3); the book says there is no ironclad defence (p.706-707).
- The worked examples rely on chart plates whose lettered points were not read as images (OCR noise); the spec uses the text only.
