## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
[Dow] The market always runs three movements at once: the daily move, a secondary swing, and the main movement. Each smaller move is part of a larger one (p.36, p.39). The main movement is defined by structure: it is a bull period while each high point exceeds the earlier high points, and a bear period once a low point falls below the earlier low points (p.40). Dow's trading idea is to trade with the main movement and enter on the secondary counter-move: "If the main move is up, relapses are speculators' opportunities, but if the main move is down, rallies furnish these opportunities" (p.39). His order of work is value first, then the direction of the main movement, then the position of the secondary swing (p.40). In his worked example he buys part of the line after a gradual relapse in a clear bull market. He buys more on a further decline or once price shows a "well defined advancing tendency" (p.40). He sums up his method as "buying after a period of steadiness in prices, stopping losses and letting profits run" (p.64). The "line", the double top and action/reaction appear as supporting reading methods (p.42-p.44). The book is not a chart manual. Its Dow chapters are editorials that also cover value, stops, capital and short selling.

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend [Dow]:** The main movement comes from swing highs and lows: a higher high keeps the bull period, and a low below the earlier lows marks a bear period (p.40). Dow tells the trader to keep a chart of his stocks' swings to see "where in the general swing" price stands (p.36-p.37). He also tells him to watch the general market through the averages (p.33 rule 1, p.37, p.46). He warns that the end of an advance and an unusually large secondary move look the same while they happen (p.40).
- **Secondary swing [Dow]:** This is the counter-move against the main movement and the place to act (p.39). By the "law of action and reaction", a primary move is generally followed by a secondary move the other way of at least three-eighths of it, and the bigger the move, the bigger the reaction (p.43-p.44). A "normal setback" after a 10-point general advance is about four points (p.67).
- **Levels / areas [Dow]:** The "book method" line is an active stock that holds within a narrow range (about 2 points in the example) until it forms "quite a long horizontal line". Such a line "sometimes suggests that stock has been accumulated or distributed" (p.42). In the double top, a stock tops, declines moderately, returns near the high, then recedes and "is liable to decline some distance". The book adds that traders using it alone meet many exceptions and many times when no signal is given (p.42-p.43).
- **Turn behaviour [Dow]:** After a run, a sudden reversal without "considerable fluctuations near the turning points" is unusual. After a rise there is usually a period of narrow fluctuations (p.66).
- **Volume:** [Dow] says to keep a record of the volume of transactions with the price chart (p.37), but he gives no volume rule. [XXXIV tipster] Largest volume on advances and falling volume on reactions means a bull market (p.203 hint 7).
- **Tape / other stocks [Dow]:** "Responses": when leaders are put up, the move has public support only if the rest of the list follows (p.44). This needs several charts (see section 7).
- **Dullness [XXXV unsigned]:** "In a bull market, dullness is generally followed by advances; in a bear market, by decline" (p.229-p.230).
- **Shakeout [XXVIII Nelson]:** A manipulated stock "breaks sharply" to force out margin traders, and then the advance is resumed (p.165).
- **Relapse shape [XXXV unsigned]:** After a large advance, a relapse has a quick first stage and then a slow, irregular fall (p.212). A triple top followed by a relapse is a street theory, "not sufficiently certain to justify action" alone (p.212).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown. [Dow] says that in a bear market "the whole proceeding ought to be reversed" (p.64; also p.34 rule 3, p.39).

Dow's own conditions:
- **S1 [Dow]** Main movement up: successive swing highs each exceed the previous swing highs, and no swing low has broken below the previous swing lows (p.40).
- **S2 [Dow]** Price is in a secondary relapse against that main movement: a decline from the last swing high (p.39, p.34 rule 3, p.37).
- **S3 [Dow]** The relapse is a gradual decline, not a sudden collapse ("slowly declined in sympathy with the market") (p.40; compare p.63 on a price "suddenly jammed down").
- **S4 [Dow]** The relapse has given back a sizeable part of the prior advance. The book's figure is "at least three-eighths" of the primary move (p.43-p.44). Its normal setback is about four points after a ten-point advance (p.67).
- **S5 [Dow]** After the relapse, price shows a period of steadiness: narrow fluctuations with no new low (p.64, p.66).
- **S6 [Dow]** Optional context: a long horizontal "line" of narrow-range trading in an active stock. The line can mean accumulation or distribution, and the book does not say which from the line alone (p.42).
- **S7 [Dow, needs two charts]** The general market, as shown by the averages, is in the same main movement (bull period) as the stock, and it has also had its "normal setback" (p.33 rule 1, p.37, p.46, p.67). This needs the stock chart plus a chart of the averages.

Supporting reads from non-Dow chapters (labelled; not part of Dow's setup):
- **S8 [XXXV unsigned]** In a bull period, the pullback goes dull: quiet, narrow bars on low activity. In a bull market such dullness is "generally followed by advances" (p.229-p.230).
- **S9 [XXXIV tipster]** Volume is largest on the advances and falls off on the reactions (p.203 hint 7).
- **S10 [XXXIV tipster]** After a decline, many fluctuations over a narrow range, with lows that hold (p.202 hints 4 and 6). After a rally, the later pullbacks give back smaller and smaller parts of each rally (p.202 hint 2).

Two-average confirmation (choice made): **listed in section 7, not coded as a condition.** This book's Dow text never states that two averages must confirm each other. It speaks only of "the averages" as the general market guide (p.33, p.37, p.46). S7 records that guide as a "two charts" condition (stock vs averages). The railroad-industrial confirmation rule comes from Hamilton (see DOW_HAMILTON_1922).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
**[Dow]** Once the main movement is clearly up and the stock has relapsed from its last top, buy part of the intended line, not all of it. The book measures the relapse in points: begin buying after "four or five points decline from the last previous top" (p.34 rule 3). Its examples are a four-point fall from the previous highest (p.37) and a slow decline from 108 to 98 (p.40). Buy more "if the stock had further decline or if the price showed a well defined advancing tendency", then watch the general market and wait for an advance (p.40). For the out-of-town trader, wait until the general market "has had its normal setback from a high point" before the first purchase (p.67). Dow states the principle as "buying after a period of steadiness in prices" (p.64). Bear side: begin selling after "three or four points rally from the bottom" in a bear market (p.34 rule 3), and otherwise reverse the whole procedure (p.64).

The book's sizes are in absolute points for the stocks of its time. A chart reader can use only the relative shape: a relapse of a sizeable part of the prior swing (S4), then steadiness (S5) or an advancing tendency.

Not Dow (labelled, reported only): [XXXIV tipster] gives a breakout signal: price advancing "beyond this range on heavy transactions" after narrow fluctuations following a decline (p.202 hint 4). It also says that after dullness, a stock that "begins to advance on heavy transactions" should be bought "for a three days' rise" (p.203 hint 10). These are not part of Dow's trigger.

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- **[Dow]** A swing low falls below the previous swing lows: the bull period has ended and a bear period begins (p.40).
- **[Dow]** After the purchase, the price falls further beyond the expected relapse. Dow says this suggests "a down swing of larger proportions than had been anticipated" (p.51). If the stock goes down, "the theory on which the purchase was made was wrong" (p.29).
- **[Dow]** Double top: a top, a moderate decline, a return near the high, then a renewed decline means price "is liable to decline some distance". The book warns that this has many exceptions (p.42-p.43).
- **[Dow]** Caution, not proof: what looks like the end of the advance may be only an unusually large secondary move (p.40).
- **[XXXV unsigned]** A triple top followed by a relapse suggests that the forces checking the advance are strong at that level. The book adds that this is not enough to act on alone (p.212).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- [Dow] "Cut your losses short, but let your profits run" (p.29). Stop losses within two or three points of the purchase price for trades made on points, tips or impressions (p.47, p.51).
- [Dow] Stop handling: after a 5-point reaction buy, take a further 2-point fall as the stop. On re-buying, set the stop about 3 points away and keep it 3 points below the market until the stock shows "toppiness". Then tighten it to 2 points (p.51-p.52). A stop matters most at entry and in pyramiding, and least when a well-bought stock is slowly advancing (p.52).
- [Dow] Let profits run until the general market "shows a decided change of temper", or trail a stop about 3 points behind the highs (p.63). After a 2-3 point rise, put in a stop about two points back from the top and follow the rise with it (p.67-p.68).
- [Dow] A semi-investor on 50% margin, trading with the main tendency on value, needs no stop order (p.46).
- [Dow] Profit expectation: do not buy unless a rise of four or five points is expected (p.66). In his example, after a 10-point decline expect a recovery of more than 5 points, and think about stops after 5 points (p.40-p.41).
- [Dow] Capital and size: $1,000 is not enough for 100-share lots on 10% margin. A $2,500 capital fits a ten-share scale, buying every point down from 5 points below the top (p.32-p.35). Overtrading is the main danger (p.54-p.57).
- [Dow] Scale buying is a separate method. It means buying a good stock every point down on value and holding until recovery (p.31-p.34, p.60).
- [XXVII Financial Record] "Average up" rather than "average down" (p.149).
- [XXXV unsigned] Stop about two points from the highest during a manipulated campaign (p.229). [XXXIV tipster] Limit losses to half a point to 2 points, or else buy on scale down (p.203 hint 11).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- [Dow] Time durations: daily, then "two weeks to a month or more", then "at least four years" (p.36). Secondary "ten days to sixty days", main swing "four to six years" (p.39). Bull and bear markets "four and five years" (p.33). Not coded.
- [Dow] "Theory of averages" (counting days of advance vs decline) is a day count, and Dow himself rejects it (p.43). Not coded.
- [Dow] Recurrence of crises, the ten-year crisis list and business conditions (p.86-p.89). [XXXV unsigned] Five-year trade cycle and business cycles (p.214, p.224-p.226). Not coded.
- [Dow] Value first: earnings, dividends and value of the stock (p.34 rule 2, p.37, p.40, p.45, p.59-p.62, p.67, p.75). These are not on a price chart.
- [Dow] Responses: whether the rest of the list follows the leaders (p.44, also p.180-p.181). This needs many charts and is not coded.
- **Two-average confirmation:** not stated in this book (see section 3). Placed here by choice. The book uses "the averages" only as the general market guide (p.33, p.37, p.46).
- [XXXIV tipster] Day and time rules: "third day of an advance", three-day rises and resting days, extreme dullness for "a day or two", "some weeks" at a low level (p.202-p.203). Campaign timing and seasons (p.205). [XXXV unsigned] Moves of "three or four days" (p.212). Panics lasting "parts of three days", recovery "within a week" or thirty days (p.217). Not coded.
- News, rumors, financial criticism and tips (p.50-p.51, p.90-p.102, p.140-p.147, p.199-p.205). Following manipulators and public-opinion contrarian rules (p.28-p.29, p.199-p.201). Not coded.
- Short-selling mechanics, loaning rates, squeezes and corners (p.69-p.73, p.168-p.173). Brokers, bucket shops, discretionary accounts and liability (p.78-p.85, p.111-p.139). Not chart items.

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- Dow's trigger is stated in absolute points (4-5 points from the top, a 3-point stop) for 1900s railroad stocks. A chart reading can use only the relative shape (S4, S5). How to scale "points" to other instruments: **Book is silent.**
- Dow puts value first (p.40). A chart-only test drops that step, so it does not test the whole of Dow's method.
- Book is silent on bars or candles. "Well defined advancing tendency" (p.40) is not defined further.
- The book does not say which a line is (accumulation or distribution) until price leaves it. The breakout rule here (p.202) is the tipster's, not Dow's.
- Attribution: the preface says IV-XX are Dow's (p.7), but the chapter marks cover V-XIX. Ch XXXV items are unsigned except p.226 (Dow), p.216 (Bennett), p.223 (West) and p.227 (Kellogg).
- OCR: fractions are lost on p.34 rule 4 (rally "% per cent to % per cent" of the decline) and p.49. The book's stated rally fraction is therefore unreadable.
- S7 needs a second chart (the averages). A single chart image cannot check it.
