# CWMORGAN_SPECULATE_1900: How to Speculate Successfully in Wall Street (C. W. Morgan & Co., c1900)
Book file: books/public-domain/32_CWMorgan_How-to-Speculate-Successfully-in-Wall-Street_c1900.pdf  |  Note: books/notes/32_CWMorgan_How-to-Speculate-Successfully.md
Status: independent book strategy; not part of Abhi's method; reference/testing only.

Source warning: the book is a brokerage firm's promotional pamphlet (C. W. Morgan & Co., members of the New York Consolidated Stock Exchange, p.5-p.6, p.33-p.41). The strategy below is the pamphlet's own "chart theory" section (p.31-p.33), which it presents as the theory of "chart students", with one illustrative chart (People's Gas). It is not a tested method.

## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
The pamphlet says charts are not exact; their use is to show quickly when the trader is on the wrong side, so he can take a small loss (p.31). The theory: after a stock advances into new ground and reacts moderately, it returns to the high and makes another top before a lasting decline (double top, a sell signal); after it falls into new low ground, is checked and recovers somewhat, it comes back very near or to the old low (double bottom, a buy signal) (p.31). The "chart law" for being wrong: a break a full point under an old bottom means the support is gone, so the long is closed and a short is opened; after that break the stock is not a buy again until new double bottoms form (p.32). In the People's Gas example each signal both closes the old position and opens the opposite one (p.32). The pamphlet sums it up as the old saying "buy on the bottom, sell on the top" (p.32). The chart is a point-scale line drawn from the opening price, showing only full-point moves, and the trader also watches other stocks of the same class (p.33).

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** no formal definition. The pattern context is a move "into new territory" (up) or "new low territory" (down) (p.31). The example is read as a sequence of tops and bottoms (p.32).
- **Levels:** prior bottoms act as "support"; breaking under them removes it (p.32). Lines/groups of tops (B, H, K, M) and lines of bottoms (C, D, F-G, I-J) mark the turns (p.32). The "accumulating point" is a level after a severe break that traders regard as a safe buy (p.29); the example's later decline returns to "the old accumulating point" at 92 (p.32).
- **Pattern quality:** described only in words: "good line of tops", "excellent line of bottoms", "much more perfect bottom", bottoms "not as good as they might be", "excellent tops" (p.32). No measure is given.
- **Volume:** Book is silent in the chart section. Outside it, the successful trader reads a top when demand "appears to be quickly satisfied" after a 10-15 point rise, taken as unloading (p.9). This is a tape description, not a chart rule.
- **Bars/tape:** the chart is a single price line from the opening, one grid space per point, full-point moves only; smaller moves are "nervous fluctuations" (p.33).
- **Other stocks:** watch the charts of other stocks of the same class; leave a stock that turns dull or uncertain for one whose chart "makes a more definite assertion" (p.33).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown; the book states the short side as the mirror (double tops "just as reliable signals to sell on", p.31).

- **S1** Price has declined into new low ground (below the recent lows) (p.31).
- **S2** The decline has been checked and followed by "something of a recovery" (p.31).
- **S3** Price then falls again "very near, or quite to" the previous low and holds there, forming a double bottom. In the example a triple bottom and a fourth bottom (I, J) also count, the later one "much more perfect" (p.31-p.32).
- **S4** Short-side mirror: price has advanced into new ground, reacted or declined moderately, then returned to the high point, forming a double top or a line/group of tops at about one level (p.31-p.32). In the example most traders waited for the second top to reach the level of the earlier tops (one point higher than E, at 109) rather than selling below it (p.32).
- **S5** After a break under an old bottom (see section 5), no new long setup exists until double bottoms form again (p.32).
- **S6** Optional context: the stock is not dull or erratic compared with other stocks of the same class (p.33). Needs charts of the group.
- **S7** Chart construction: only moves of a full point or more are counted; smaller moves are ignored (p.33). The book's "point" is $1 per share on 1899-1900 prices; no scaling rule is given.

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
- **Double bottom formed:** buy for the long account, and cover any short at the same time (p.31, p.32 at A, C, D, G, J).
- **Double top / good line of tops formed:** sell the long, and (in the example) sell short; double tops are "just as reliable signals to sell on" (p.31, p.32 at B, H, M).
- **Break of an old bottom:** price a full point under an old bottom is "according to chart law a signal to sell out": close the long and sell short (p.32: at 106 after buying at 107; at 103½ under the D bottoms).
- The book states the break rule only for bottoms. A break above an old top as a buy signal: **Book is silent.**
- Book's distances as printed (1900 stock prices, no scaling rule): the break is "a full point" under the old bottom; the example's loss on each wrong trade is one point (p.32).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- Long: price breaks a full point under the old bottom (the double bottom it was bought on); support is "removed" and the trader is "on the wrong side of the market" (p.32).
- Long held too long: price falls a full point under the double bottoms instead of being sold at the level of earlier tops ("mistake number two") (p.32).
- The book names the purpose of charts as exactly this: to show quickly when the trader is wrong so he takes a small loss (p.31).
- Short: closed when double bottoms form (p.32). A break above an old top as a stop for shorts: **Book is silent.**
- After a severe decline with no double bottom, the read is further decline, not a buy (p.32).
- The book itself warns that charts are not absolutely accurate and shows two mistaken trades in its own example (p.31-p.32).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Chart exit = the opposite signal: longs are sold at double tops/lines of tops, shorts covered at double bottoms (p.32). Loss exit = a full point under the old bottom, one-point loss in the example (p.32).
- Worked order example: stop-loss two points under the purchase price; it limits the loss in a 12-point break to 2 points (p.13-p.15). Sample orders: buy at 66, stop 2 points under, sell at 69; short at 35, stop over the sale price (fraction lost in OCR), cover at a 3-point decline (p.16). A profit order closed out at about 1½ points (p.15).
- The successful trader sells after a 10-15 point climb when the stock loses popularity (p.9). Play for big profits and small losses (p.10-p.11).
- Margin: 10 points' protection "is generally considered a safe margin" (p.14); $500 margin on 50 shares (p.20); margin calls when price is within 3-4 points of the exhaust point (p.21); example of re-buying lower after a margin call (p.21). Securities as margin, sized at 10 points per share (p.12).
- Size: the main cause of failure is trying to make too much too fast and plunging (p.7-p.9). Lots: 10 shares minimum on margin, multiples of 10 (p.38).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)

- Chart drawn from "where the stock opened that morning" (session-based construction) (p.33). Not coded as a reading rule.
- Order timing: orders void after the 3 P.M. close unless "good until cancelled"; opening orders (p.13, p.15).
- Monday settling and weekly interest; 6% yearly interest plan (p.20, p.38).

- "A good income regularly every month", doubling capital in months, "fortunes every week", a dull month most years (p.7-p.9, p.40).
- Beginners checking "the last month or so" of a stock's record (p.10).
- News and discounting the news; "buy when things look darkest, sell when all looks bright" (p.24-p.25).
- Manipulation, cliques, money-trust call-money rates, earnings vs price as proof of manipulation (p.23-p.24).
- Dividends and "X dividend" effects; dividend checks by beginners (p.10, p.22-p.23).
- Tips: "copper" them (p.30). Buying after the cause of a decline is removed (p.9).
- Dull spells tend to decline (p.19): activity, no chart threshold given.
- Brokerage promotion: Consolidated Exchange advantages, commissions, market bulletin (p.20, p.33-p.41).

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- Promotional pamphlet: one illustrative chart, no test results; the firm calls charts "wonderfully accurate" while also saying they are not absolutely accurate (p.31, p.33).
- Fixed distances ("a full point" break, one-point losses, the 1-point chart unit) come from 1899-1900 prices (People's Gas about 90-115). **No scaling rule is given.**
- "Moderately", "something of a recovery", "very near" and pattern quality ("good", "excellent", "more perfect") have no numeric definition (p.31-p.32).
- The break rule is stated only for old bottoms; the upside break of an old top: **Book is silent** (p.32).
- The chart is a point line from the opening, not bars or candles (p.33); how to apply it to a bar/candle chart: **Book is silent.** No volume rule in the chart section.
- S6 needs charts of other stocks of the same class; one chart image cannot check it.
- OCR: the chart pages (p.31-p.33) mix two columns and were read from rendered images; fractions are often lost (e.g. the short-stop size on p.16).
