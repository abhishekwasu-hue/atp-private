# WYCKOFF_TAPE_1910: Studies in Tape Reading (Richard D. Wyckoff, writing as "Rollo Tape", 1910)
Book file: books/public-domain/01_Wyckoff_Studies-in-Tape-Reading_1910.pdf  |  Note: books/notes/01_Wyckoff_Studies-in-Tape-Reading.md
Status: independent book strategy; not part of Abhi's method; reference/testing only.

All page refs are PDF pages (printed page = PDF page - 6).

## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
The book teaches the trader to follow the immediate trend by weighing supply against demand from what the ticker tape prints: prices, the size of the lots traded, and how price responds to buying or selling pressure (p.15-16, p.81-82). Prices move along the line of least resistance. Supply placed in the path of a move is either absorbed, and price goes through, or it overcomes demand, and price turns back (p.66-67, p.93). The book gives two entries of its own. The first is to go with a stock the moment it leaves a narrow, dull range, above all when volume expands (p.96, p.123, p.160-163). The second is to buy the first reaction after a move has started, then judge from how the stock acts there whether the read holds (p.161, p.169). In a valid move, volume swells on the advances and dries up on the reactions, and each swing reaches a new extreme (p.96-97, p.169-170). The book is a tape method. It has no candles or bars and uses charts only as a side tool (p.132-144). The conditions below are the parts of the book that can be seen on a price/volume chart. Tape-only evidence, such as whether lots were taken at the offer or hit at the bid, is listed in section 7.

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** consecutive higher tops and bottoms, "like stairs going up", make an up trend; repeated steps to lower levels make a down trend (p.70, p.141). The trader goes with the immediate trend and never against it (p.66-67). He also keeps his bearings in the larger swings (p.112).
- **Trendless market:** a market that swings within a couple of points "cannot be said to have a trend" and is one to avoid while it stays that way (p.67).
- **Levels ("points of resistance"):** the levels where the market turns after a reaction (p.64). A row of tops or bottoms at the same level shows resistance (p.141). A low that stops above the earlier low means the point of resistance has been raised, so demand is more urgent (p.69).
- **Ranges:** a long zig-zag in a narrow band on very small volume means lifelessness. The same zig-zag on normal or large volume points to accumulation or distribution (p.141). Dullness means the forces are in balance, and a pronounced swing follows (p.116-118).
- **Volume:** volume is judged against the stock's normal activity (p.84). In a bull move, trading swells on the advances and shrinks on the reactions (p.96-97, p.169-170). Heavy volume on a decline, followed by a sluggish rally on small lots, shows liquidation (p.96). Very large volume inside a small price band, with no progress, shows supply meeting demand (p.73-75, p.163).
- **Response to pressure:** strength or weakness is read from what price does and does not do at critical points (p.101, p.104). A rally that falls short of a normal recovery means the decline is not over (p.112, p.150).
- **Move life:** volume, activity and range widen as a move goes on, and the end comes in "riotous conditions" (p.185-186).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
The book describes two entry contexts: (A) leaving a dull or narrow range, and (B) the first reaction inside a move that has started. S1 applies to both. S2-S4 describe context A, S5-S7 describe context B, and S8-S10 are the book's warning and turn readings.

- **S1 Swing trend:** the chart shows consecutive higher tops and higher bottoms (up) or lower tops and lower bottoms (down). The setup is taken in that direction (p.66-67, p.70, p.141).
- **S2 Range with volume:** price moves sideways in a narrow band (a "protracted zig-zag within a short radius"). Volume inside the band is normal or large, which the book reads as accumulation or distribution; very small volume means lifeless (p.141; p.116-119).
- **S3 Hardening inside the range:** inside the range the bottoms are gradually raised, and a dip stops above the earlier low (bullish). Gradually lowering tops are the bearish mirror (p.69, p.119, p.135-136).
- **S4 Range boundaries:** two or more tops, or two or more bottoms, at about the same price mark the range edges, i.e. the points of resistance (p.64, p.123, p.141).
- **S5 Volume with the move:** after the move starts, volume bars are larger on the swings in the trend direction than on the reactions against it (p.96-97, p.169-170).
- **S6 Light-volume reaction:** the first reaction after the move has started is small compared with the advance before it and comes on light volume. The book reads this as a natural reaction, not a change of trend; its example is 1 to 1.5 points back after a 3-point rise (p.161, p.169).
- **S7 Reaction holds the point of resistance:** the reaction low stays above the prior swing low / point of resistance (p.69-70).
- **S8 Supply at the top (warning, bearish):** near a high, very large volume trades in a narrow price band with little or no price progress, after which price falls back (p.73-75, p.91-93, p.163).
- **S9 Liquidation pattern (bearish):** declines come on large volume, and the following rallies come on small volume and recover less than a normal part of the drop. The book's normal rally is one-half to two-thirds of the decline (p.96, p.112, p.150).
- **S10 Exhaustion turn (bullish):** after a sharp decline, a final push lower comes on clearly lighter volume than the earlier selling, followed by up-bars on rising volume (p.147-148).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
The book acts on any one of three signals. It acts promptly; it states that "seconds are often more valuable than minutes" (p.66).
1. **Range exit (main signal):** a line is drawn at the high and at the low of the narrow range, and the trader buys or sells "whenever the line is crossed" (p.123). When a stock that has moved within a narrow band suddenly "takes on new life and activity, bursting through its former bounds, he must go with it" (p.160-161). A sudden jump in volume inside the range shows a new movement has started. The book adds caution until price "breaks through its former limits with large and increasing volumes" (p.162-163). A break through strong resistance means the resistance is used up, so "it is best to go with it" (p.96).
2. **First reaction:** the trader either "gets in at the inception of a movement or waits for the first reaction after the move has started", knows where the reaction should come, and judges from the stock's action there whether his first read is confirmed (p.169). He repurchases on the reaction (p.161). In chart terms this is S5 + S6 + S7.
3. **Turn after a final drive:** after a long decline, a final drive on little volume is followed by larger buying, and he buys during that drive (p.148). On the short side, a stock that will not rally while others try, then breaks "through with a thud" on large volume, is the signal to sell (p.106-107).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- After entry the trade "must come up to expectations or he should abandon the trade". In a bull move the volume must increase, the reactions must show smaller volume than the advances, and each upward swing must last longer and reach a new high. Otherwise "the rise has spent its force" (p.169-170).
- Price breaks back through the last point of resistance / the reaction low (the level the book uses for its stop) (p.64, p.69-70).
- A stock that cannot be advanced is assumed to decline next; "a market seldom stands still" (p.74).
- A rally that falls short of a normal recovery, or that comes on small volume, shows the decline is not over (p.112, p.150).
- A volume burst inside a range does not fix the direction; the first direction may be false. Abnormally large volume within a small price band marks a "fictitious" move (p.162-163).
- Gradually lowering tops in a range mean that pressure has resumed (p.136).
- A move that widens into fast, high-volume, "riotous" action is at its end (p.185-186).
- Being shaken out right after entry "does not prove his judgment in error" (p.70).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Stops: a two-point maximum gross loss is the "arbitrary" stop for times when the trader is away from the tape (p.64). Closer stop: just beyond the last point of resistance (p.64, p.69-70). Automatic trailing stop: always one point from the extreme price until commissions are covered (p.64). The stop is raised to cost plus commission once the stock passes the prior top, then to each new point of resistance (p.70). In a 3-point rise the stop is pushed up behind until the first good reaction takes him out near the high (p.161). The book's other stop types (the "double" stop-and-reverse and mental stops) are listed in the note at p.62-71, p.126, p.166-168, p.178-179.
- Exits: he sells as price again nears the prior high unless it looks set to forge through (p.161). He aims to get in at a move's "Infancy" and out at its "Old Age" (p.161). When it is time to close, "it is usually time to reverse" (p.73). Volume and activity widen toward the end of a move (p.185-186).
- R:R: since a trade costs nearly half a point, "each risk should contain a probable two to five points profit, or it is not justified" (p.67). Purchases with "only a fractional risk" (p.162).
- Size: commitments no greater than one's susceptibility to fear allows (p.66). Start small, grow the unit with capital, compound small average profits (note: p.27-29, p.157, p.175-178). Pyramiding on a sustained advance (note: p.130-131).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- Time rules: close every trade before 3 p.m. / no overnight positions (p.19, p.36, p.157); the "hourly cycle" with clock times (p.114-115); tighter stops in the last fifteen minutes (p.167); aim at the moves of the next half hour (p.184); no more than three or four days in one direction (p.114); a 25-point break "at least once a year" in a bull market (p.112); ranges lasting "for days at a time" (p.162).
- Tape-only evidence that an OHLCV chart does not show: lots taken at the offer vs hit at the bid (p.81-89, p.121, p.148); jumps between consecutive sales (p.97, p.150); lot sizes (100-share vs thousands) (p.121, p.162).
- Other stocks and the whole market: leaders, whether the rest of the market follows, and the cue given by another stock (p.44-59, p.73-78, p.106-107, p.122); the composite average chart (p.140).
- News, insiders, pools, manipulators, wash sales and floor traders (p.84-89, p.98, p.105, p.110-111, p.119, p.163).
- Stock selection, brokers, market orders, commissions (p.33-59, p.173-175).
- Counting figure-chart columns to project a move (p.138).

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- The book is a tape method. Mapping its tape events (lots at the offer, jumps between sales) onto chart bars is not done in the book. Only the chart-visible parts are coded, so S5, S6, S10 and the trigger rely on bar volume and stand in for the tape evidence.
- Volume is "large" or "light" only relative to the stock's normal activity (p.84). The book gives no number.
- Fixed figures in the book (1 to 1.5 points after 3, a normal rally of one-half to two-thirds) come from 1909 stock prices. The coded text keeps them only as the book's own ratios (S6, S9).
- The book contradicts itself, and these notes do not resolve it. p.15 says tape reading is not "buying on dips and selling on bulges" or trading chart indications, yet p.161 and p.169 buy the first reaction and p.134-136 use a chart. p.67 says to avoid a market without a trend, while p.116-123 trade the exit from a dull range.
- The book says the chart gives the direction and the tape gives the moment (p.138). A single-stock chart misses what the rest of the market does (p.139).
- OCR: fractions in tape prices are garbled. p.83 and p.86 were read from images (see the note). Only round levels and the text descriptions are used here.
