# DICE_STOCKMARKET_1926: The Stock Market (Charles Amos Dice, 1926; scan of the 4th printing, 1928)
Book file: books/public-domain/35_Dice_The-Stock-Market_1926-4th-printing-1928.pdf  |  Note: books/notes/35_Dice_The-Stock-Market.md
Status: independent book strategy; not part of Abhi's method; reference/testing only.

This is the tape reader's and chart trader's method **as described by Dice** in Chapter XV (with the matching stop example in Chapter XIII and the action-of-the-market passages in Chapter XVIII). Dice reports it; he does not recommend it as his own (p.313-p.315). Page refs are PDF pages (PDF page = printed page + 22). Wording is a paraphrase unless quoted.

## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
As described by Dice, the tape reader works on supply and demand: a move runs, then supply and demand reach a balance, then one side wins again, and the skill lies in reading those resting points (p.305). Recent tops and bottoms are "resistance points" where buying support or selling pressure appeared (p.306). The signal to sell is a double top followed by a third rally that fails to reach the former tops, while other leaders also weaken; the signal to buy is a double bottom followed by a decline that stops above the previous lows, while other leaders are not weak (p.306-p.307; Figure 6, p.279-p.280). Volume is read alongside: larger volume at higher prices suggests accumulation, at lower prices distribution; an unusually large volume often marks the turn; heavy volume that brings little further advance shows supply overbalancing demand (p.308-p.309). The full-point figure chart shows where stock was offered and where demand outran supply; in the book's example, a break above the demand row after the low and a reaction that held above the prior level signalled the advance, and a hesitation was expected at the former top (p.309-p.310). The chart is only an indicator of turns and is not followed unless the tape confirms it (p.310).

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** the tape reader trades inside the larger swings of 5 to 15 points. If the trend for some time has been up 10-12 points and the market shows a temporary turn, he expects a reaction of 5-6 points, and after it a move above the point reached before the reaction (p.303). Book figures in 1920s points; no scaling rule given.
- **Levels:** recent tops (selling pressure) and bottoms (buying support) are resistance points (p.306); double and triple tops and bottoms (p.306-p.307); figure-chart rows where much stock was offered or demand outran supply (p.309-p.310); a sideways "trading area" between a line across the tops and a line across the bottoms (p.281); in the Dow chapter, the "line" as accumulation or distribution (p.515-p.516).
- **Volume:** larger volumes at higher prices = accumulation; at lower prices = distribution (p.308). A comparatively large volume often marks the turning point: a sudden run-up on greatly increasing volume is often the top of the move; a sudden large supply driving price to a resistance point puts the turn in sight (p.308). A much larger buying volume with little advance, then a stop and some weakness, means supply is overbalancing demand (p.308-p.309). Longer view: very large volume over a long period goes with distribution, small volume with accumulation (p.378-p.379).
- **Bar/tape:** the tape is primary and the chart is an aid to memory (p.309-p.310). A rapid run-up after a period of small swings soon meets supply, and a hesitation with no further advance points to a reaction; a rapid decline soon meets demand, and a hesitation near the previous low with little further decline points to support (p.381).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown; the book gives the mirror for the short side (p.306, p.280).
- **S1 - Resistance points marked:** the chart shows recent swing tops and swing bottoms at which price turned (p.306).
- **S2 - Double bottom:** two declines end at about the same price (p.306-p.307; Figure 7 p.307; Figure 6 points A and B p.279-p.280).
- **S3 - Higher third low:** the next decline after the double bottom meets support and stops above the previous lows (p.306-p.307; Figure 6 points C and D p.279-p.280).
- **S4 - Volume points to accumulation:** volume is larger on the rises than on the declines, or the last decline into the low is stopped by unusually large volume (p.308).
- **S5 - Figure-chart base (optional reading):** after the low, price rises above the level where demand earlier outran supply, and the following reaction holds above the level it came from (book example: up through 21, reaction from 23 held at 22) (p.309-p.310).
- **S6 - Trading area (optional reading):** in a sideways market, a horizontal line joins the bottoms and another joins the tops; price in the lower part of the area, or closing out of the top of it (p.281).
- **S7 - Other leaders agree (needs two charts):** other leading stocks (or the averages) are not weak, or show the same pattern; for a trading-area breakout, the averages break out too (p.306-p.307, p.281).
- **S8 - Short-side mirror:** a double top, then a third rally that fails to reach the former tops, with larger volume at the lower prices or heavy volume bringing little advance (p.306, p.308-p.309).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
As described by Dice: after a double bottom, when the next decline meets enough resistance to stop above the previous lows, Steel "is about to go up" and the trader goes long at once; in the stop example the trader "takes the cue and buys" at the higher low (p.306-p.307, p.279-p.280). Mirror: after a double top, when the third attempt fails to reach the former tops and other leaders also weaken, "it is time to sell" (p.306). The chart signal is acted on only when the tape seconds it (p.310).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- Other leaders are weak enough to drag the stock down despite its double bottom (p.307).
- Price returns below the double-bottom lows (the book's stop sits one-eighth below them) (p.279-p.280).
- In a trading area, price leaves the area on the side against the position (p.281).
- The tape does not second the chart's forecast (p.310).
- After an advance, larger volume brings little progress and the advance stops, then weakens (supply overbalancing demand) (p.308-p.309); a sudden run-up on greatly increasing volume often marks the end of the move (p.308).
- Dice's own caution: a hesitating rise on increased volume during a long advance can be mistaken for final distribution (p.379).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Office trader's stop (Figure 6): buy at the higher low, stop one-eighth below the double-bottom lows; once price is far enough that a reaction will not reach the entry ("flat" point), move the stop there; after each reaction move the stop to within an eighth or a quarter below its low; sell after a double top (p.279-p.280).
- Trading-area stop: just below the previous bottoms (buyer) or just above the previous tops (seller) (p.281).
- The "two-point stop" is called a mechanical rule with no place in that method; the stop may be two points or five or more depending on where the stock was bought (p.282).
- After a long rise a reaction of half the advance may be expected; a following stop must allow for it (p.282).
- Tape reader's scale: satisfied with one-eighth, seldom tries for more than two or three points; small profits, minute losses, large turnover (p.303).
- Pyramid: stop at or below the original buying price until the advance is 6-8 points; after 10-15 points expect a 5-7 point reaction (p.326-p.327).
- All figures are 1920s stock points (eighths of a dollar); the book gives no scaling rule.

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- Tape reader closes out each day and holds nothing overnight; hour-to-hour trading (p.302-p.303). Not coded.
- Trend frame "for perhaps the last two weeks or month" (p.303); short swings of "2 to 6 or 8 weeks" (p.380); hesitation "for 2 to 3 days" and declines "in about 10 trading days" (p.381). Not coded (C.4); only the price shapes are used.
- Chart men's "time factor", dated targets in red ink, and "Hebrew chronology" (p.313). Not coded.
- News as a test (strong on bad news = strong hands) (p.304, p.381-p.382). Not chart.
- Tests of the market by selling blocks (p.380); tape-only reciprocal influences between stocks in real time (p.304, p.309). Not chart.
- Monthly average volume figures for past distribution and accumulation periods (p.378-p.379). Historical, dated; only the general idea is in section 2.
- Trend-indicator services with secret formulas (p.311, p.382). Not available.
- Insiders, pools and manipulation (p.312, p.314-p.315). Only their chart effects (accumulation and distribution) are coded.

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- The method is the practice of tape readers and chart traders **as described by Dice**, not his own recommendation; he reports that tape readers and investors say no pure chart trader succeeds, and he favours the major-swing, fundamentals approach (p.313-p.316).
- The tape is primary for the tape reader and the chart is only an aid (p.309-p.310); a chart-only test cannot check the "seconded by the tape" step.
- The full-point figure chart needs every transaction (p.309); it cannot be rebuilt exactly from candles, so S5 is an approximation on a candle chart.
- "About the same price" for a double top or bottom, "comparatively large" and "unusual" volume have no numbers. The book's examples use fractions of a point on 1920s prices (p.306-p.307). Book is silent on tolerances and scaling.
- Garbled fractions in the text layer of p.306 were read from the page image; see the note, section 8.
