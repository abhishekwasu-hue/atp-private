## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
**This book does not contain a complete chart strategy.** It is mostly about investing: bonds, bank stocks, mining and oil shares, industry analysis, averaging and diversification (p.63-102, p.105-113, p.147-163, p.210-211). For method details it refers the reader back to "Studies in Tape Reading" (p.27, p.48). The price-action content is short. Trend is the main factor (p.58, p.107). A campaign moves through accumulation, mark-up and distribution (p.49). The market is judged "from its own action" (p.38, p.48, p.142). "Technical position" is the "spark" that times a purchase once the other factors line up (p.113, p.209). On the chart the book's own reading comes down to this: trade with the broad trend. Prefer a stock that has reacted after an advance and now shows "strong resistance to pressure" at the lower level over one still in its distribution range near the top (p.113-114). A stock that holds a range while the market is weak, then advances rapidly on greatly increased volume, is the mark-up stage (p.128). The book does not define a chart entry signal, a swing definition or a volume rule. It says volume is not necessary for its daily judgment (p.142-143). The conditions below are limited to what the text supports. Nothing has been added to make the strategy complete.

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** the trend is "the main factor". Working with it gives "three or four times" the chances (p.58). The long trend is "the compass by which all courses should be steered" (p.107). Overbought or oversold conditions most affect the direction of the next swing (p.27). The book does not define how to read trend on a chart (no swing-high/low rule).
- **Three movements (Dow):** a long trend, intermediate swings and small swings run at the same time (p.38). The book defines them by duration; see section 7.
- **Levels / areas:** accumulation happens inside a range, e.g. "within a range of from 50 to 60" (p.49). Pools "accumulate a stock without advancing its price; then ... bid the stock up" (p.115). At the low level of 43.5 to 46, American Graphophone "showed excellent resistance, while the rest of the market remained weak" (p.128). The distribution range sits near the top after a mark-up and is "very active around the top" (p.114). Graphophone worked "back and forth between 70 and 75" on heavy volume while insiders sold (p.128-129). Distribution may continue on the way down from the top (p.49-50).
- **Response to pressure:** a stock that rose from 100 to 150, reacted to 110 under a bear attack, and "shows at that level strong resistance to pressure" is preferred (p.113-114). A stock is weak on the bull side when buying power is exhausted after an advance. It is strong for the bears' opponents when demand overcomes supply (p.116-117).
- **Volume:** the mark-up came with "the volume of trading greatly increased" (p.128). Otherwise the book says the high, low and last prices are enough and that volume "would not be necessary", though in some instances it "might help" (p.142-143).
- **Bars / tape:** the book judges from daily high, low and last prices (p.142). It describes no bar or candle pattern.

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
- **S1 Trade with the broad trend:** the broad price direction on the chart is up for a long position, down for a short (p.58, p.107). The book does not say how to measure the trend on a chart.
- **S2 Accumulation range:** price moves sideways in a range after a decline and holds without advancing (p.49, p.115).
- **S3 Holding after a reaction:** after an advance, price reacts and then stops and holds at a lower level instead of continuing down ("strong resistance to pressure") (p.113-114).
- **S4 Holding while the market is weak:** the stock holds its level while a market-average chart for the same period is falling. This needs both charts (p.128).
- **S5 Not in the distribution range:** price is not in a back-and-forth range near the top of a large mark-up with very active trading. The book prefers S3 to that case (p.114, p.128-129).
- **S6 Mark-up with volume:** a rapid advance out of the holding range on clearly larger volume than before (p.128, p.49).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
**The book gives no defined chart trigger.** It says the technical position will "point out the exact moment when it should be bought", but it never describes what that moment looks like (p.209). Its own trading is "watching carefully for an important turning point, limiting my risk" (p.140). On U.S. Steel it waited for the stock "to get into position where I expected such a sharp upward or downward move" (p.53). Both are stated without a visible signal. The nearest concrete act is Magma Copper. After "careful buying" and a hold at the same price "for a day or two", the stock was suddenly bid higher, and he bought at the market because he likes to buy something "hard to purchase" (p.96). In chart terms this is a sudden up-move out of a holding level (S2/S3 into S6). It is recorded as the book's nearest example, not as a defined rule.

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- The book states no explicit chart invalidation. Its closest statements:
- A trade that goes "two or three points" against the entry shows that the "first judgment was wrong" (the speculator described on p.55). The size is a stop setting; see section 6.
- A long position held while the trend is down can only be exited "on the incidental rally" (p.58).
- The stock is still in the distribution range near the top after a large mark-up. The book prefers not to buy it (p.114). When the action shows insiders selling in the top range, "it is time for outsiders to sell" (p.128-130).
- After an advance has used up buying power, the stock "must decline" (p.116-117). The book describes this in terms of who holds the stock, not as a chart pattern.

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Stops: "two- or three-point stops behind any trade" made for fluctuations; Harriman used three-eighths of a point to one point (p.58). Three-point stops on each lot in the Steel campaigns (p.53-54). Livermore's mental price stop (p.139). Losses traced to not placing stops (p.140).
- R:R: "Anticipated profits should be at least three or four times the amount of the risk" (p.59).
- Exits: protect profits by moving stops up, or sell half to mark down the cost of the rest (p.59). Take the profit on a further advance or raise the stop (p.54). Partial sale in the Graphophone top range (p.128-130). Trade for the "ten or twenty point swings" (p.48, p.140).
- Size and capital: no more than "five or ten per cent" of loose capital in trading (p.52). Campaigns on $3,000 margin (p.53-54). Pyramiding: start with 300 shares and add 100 every two points up to about ten points (p.53-54). The other speculator adds a lot every point up (p.55). Averaging down on a scale, with selling on rallies (p.157-160). Diversification (p.210-211).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- Time rules: Livermore's time stop, which closes a trade if it shows no profit within two or three days (p.138-139); "the day to buy is not the day to sell" (p.141); judging the market one hour a day in mid-session (p.141-142); intermediate swings of "thirty to sixty days" (p.38, p.48, p.106, p.108); accumulation lasting "several weeks or months" (p.49); Dow's three movements defined by duration (p.38).
- Fundamentals: the six-factor checklist (industry, company trend, management, finances and earning power) (p.106-108); bonds, bank stocks, mining and oil (p.63-102, p.147-154); intrinsic value (p.59, p.159).
- Ownership and insiders: who owns the stock, pools, large interests, public participation (p.114-116, p.192-193, p.215-219); "technical position" as defined by holders and buying power (p.116-117).
- News, tips, dividends and events: Magma tip, Graphophone dividend arithmetic, newspaper articles (p.96, p.122-130).
- Buying in panics or on big declines as an investor, which is tied to intrinsic value (p.59).
- Comparison with the general market (S4) needs a second chart; it is coded only when that chart is supplied.

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- No complete chart strategy. There is no trend definition, no entry signal and no invalidation rule in chart terms. S1-S6 are the only chart-visible readings the text supports, and the trigger is an example, not a rule.
- "Technical position" is the book's timing key (p.113, p.209), but the book defines it by who holds the stock and their buying power (p.116-117). That is not visible on a chart.
- The book says volume is not necessary for its daily judgment (p.142-143), yet uses greatly increased volume in the mark-up example (p.128). Both statements are reported; neither is resolved.
- S4 needs a market-average chart alongside the stock chart. With one chart only, S4 cannot be checked.
- For a fuller chart method the book points to "Studies in Tape Reading" (p.27, p.48); see WYCKOFF_TAPE_1910, which is kept as a separate strategy.
- p.12 copyright year: the text layer reads 1926, while the title page (p.11) says 1925. The scan does not settle it (see the note).
