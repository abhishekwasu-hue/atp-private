## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
Van Riper keeps a chart of every swing of one point or more in each active stock and reads it together with the volume of transactions (p.9, p.13, p.43-p.46). His core observation: the first extreme point of a move, made on unusually large volume, is followed by a reaction and then by a **second movement** back to that same extreme, and the second test is where to act (p.10-p.11). His Rule 6 says to note the low of the day of large selling volume and, once the market starts up, to buy on the second decline to that low (mirror for tops) (p.12). If that second movement goes beyond the first extreme, the trade is stopped at a small loss (Rule 9) (p.12). A third test that stops short of the second shows the move has spent its force (p.11, p.31). His second signal is the break of an old high on large volume: cover shorts and go long, then add on reactions while tops and bottoms keep rising (p.13, p.45, p.50, Rule 10 p.12). Repeated tops at one price show liquidation (supply), repeated bottoms show support (p.49-p.50, p.77). When the chart shows nothing definite, he stays out (p.12, p.52).

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** a bull market shows each advance going beyond the last and each decline stopping above the former one, a "stepladder" of tops and bottoms (p.34, p.44, p.62-p.63). A bear wave shows each top stopping lower and the declines extending further (p.54-p.55). In a bull market declines often stop at the last previous top, and in a declining market old bottoms become new tops (p.11). Small fluctuations are "ripples" (p.44).
- **Levels:** the first extreme made on large volume (p.10-p.11); "supporting points" where price stops and rallies again and again, and "liquidating points" where it stops and falls again and again (p.48-p.49, p.54, p.77); old tops become bottoms once passed (p.11, p.64). A double top or bottom counts for more when the swing between the two tests is wide (p.64). Double tops made at a low level after a large fall are poor sell points, and vice versa (p.55).
- **Volume:** read every day with price (p.9, p.14); judged relative to the stock's normal activity (p.13). Unusually large volume marks the first extreme (p.10, p.12). Large volume and big blocks on the advances mean heavy interests are buying (p.45, p.46). Several tops at one price mean more stock for sale than buyers (p.50). A break below a support level on large volume means support has been withdrawn (p.71).
- **Bar/tape:** the chart records only moves of one point or more without a full-point reaction against them (p.43, p.46); it is a vertical-line swing chart, not a bar chart (p.38-p.39). A price half a point above a previous top seldom goes five-eighths above without going much higher (p.49). The booklet also reads tape lots and a short time rule; these are in section 7.

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown; the book applies the mirror for tops and the short side (p.10, p.12, p.64, p.75).
- **S1 - First extreme on large volume:** a sharp decline makes a new low (the "first extreme point") on a volume bar unusually large for that instrument (p.10, p.12 Rules 3 and 6).
- **S2 - Reaction:** after that low, price rallies away from it ("the market starts upward") (p.10-p.11, p.12 Rule 6).
- **S3 - Second movement to the prior extreme:** price declines again to the first extreme low, or within a small allowance of it (fractions; one to three points in wide-swinging stocks), without going clearly beyond it (p.10-p.11, p.44, p.47).
- **S4 - Wide swing between the tests:** the rally between the two lows is several points wide; the wider it is, the more the double bottom counts (p.64).
- **S5 - Third test stops short (optional strength cue):** if a third decline comes, it stops above the second low (one to three points higher), showing the decline has spent its force (p.11, p.31).
- **S6 - Rising stepladder:** after the low, each advance goes beyond the last top and each decline stops above the former bottom (p.34, p.44, p.62-p.63).
- **S7 - Volume on the advances:** volume bars are larger on the up-moves than on the down-moves, with heavy volume all the way up (p.45, p.46, p.47).
- **S8 - Old high broken on large volume:** price goes well above an old high (top) on a large volume bar (p.13, p.45, p.50, p.48 R).
- **S9 - Level context for sells:** repeated tops at one price after a long advance (double, triple or several tops) mark a liquidating point; tops made at a low level just after a large fall do not count as a sell point (p.49-p.50, p.55).
- **S10 - Other active stocks agree (needs two charts):** most active stocks show double bottoms (or tops) together; do not buy at old bottoms while the whole market is selling heavily on its declines (p.46, p.54, p.62, p.69).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
Two signals, as the book states them:
1. **Second-movement buy (Rule 6):** "On a bear movement watch for the day of large bear volumes, note the lowest figures recorded", and when the market starts upward, buy on the second downward movement to those previous low figures; on an extreme bull movement, reverse the operation (sell on the second upward movement to the prior high) (p.12; examples p.10-p.11, p.31, p.39, p.45-p.47, p.64, p.75).
2. **Old-high break (stop-and-reverse):** when an old high point is broken through with large volumes of transactions, cover any short and go long, then buy more on each reaction while tops and bottoms keep rising (Rule 10) (p.13, p.45, p.50, p.12).
The book adds the Golden Rule: when the chart shows nothing definite, do nothing (p.12, p.52, p.69).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- Rule 9: the second movement goes beyond the former extreme figures; stop the trade at a small loss and wait for a new guide (p.12, p.38).
- A purchase made at a previous bottom is sold if price breaks through that bottom (p.64, p.71, p.75).
- A short made at a double top is covered if price goes clearly beyond it (about three-quarters of a point in his examples) or well through it on large volume, and the trader goes long (p.50, p.51, p.75).
- Support withdrawn: a large volume of transactions below the support level (p.71).
- End of the up-move: a third top stops lower than the second, then each top stops lower and the declines extend further (p.54-p.55); several tops at one price after a long advance (p.49-p.50, p.62).
- The trade turns "dull and featureless" with no definite movement (exit); the time part of this rule is in section 7 (p.16).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Stops: stop losses quickly; a 2-3 point loss should not hurt the capital (p.7). Rule 9 stop beyond the former extreme (p.12). Examples: not more than half a point below the purchase (p.56); a loss of not over two points (p.55); cover at three-quarters of a point beyond the double tops (p.50). Allowance at the extremes: fractions, a point or two, two or three points in wide stocks (p.11, p.44, p.48). These are 1898-1899 stock points; the book gives no scaling rule.
- Take profits at the second top (double top) or at the next liquidating point; hold a profitable buy until a break of a point or more, then sell on the rally back near the prior top (p.45, p.63, p.67). Profit expectations: four times in five two or three points (p.48).
- Let profits run, limit losses; one good turn can repay four or five small losses (p.48, p.56, p.63, p.75).
- Size and margin: start with at least a 20-point margin ($200 for ten shares) (p.6-p.7); Rule 7: margin at least five points and preferably ten; "small loads and big margins" (p.12). Rule 10: when the market runs in your favour after an extreme and a second movement, keep buying (or selling) with the profits as margin (p.12).
- Orders: buy or sell at the market, not with limits (p.15-p.16). Averaging is rejected (p.13), though one letter allows "double up" when confident (p.29).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- "Three days" rule: in a market without a decided trend, about three days of rise then about three days of decline (p.14). Not coded (C.4).
- Exit a trade that becomes dull "within a day or so" (p.16). Only the "dull and featureless" idea is kept in section 5; the day count is not coded.
- Timing of the second movement ("within a few days", "within a week or two") (p.10-p.11, p.46). Not coded.
- Doubles formed close together in time are more reliable; old movements less reliable than new ones (p.54, p.61, p.64). Not coded (time-based).
- Same-date repetition of yearly moves, anniversaries, and ten- and twenty-year panic cycles (p.9, p.15, p.72, p.74). Not coded.
- Tape lots: 500-share and 1,000-share blocks coming out at declining prices after a few days' rise (p.14); triple bottoms within an hour (p.61). Tape-level, not on a bar chart.
- News, war, election and bank-statement comment; the 1898 market letters (p.19-p.37, p.70-p.71, p.76). Not chart.
- Insiders, pools and cliques accumulating or distributing (p.66, p.73, p.77). Only their chart effects (S7, S9) are coded.
- Commission, interest and exchange choice (p.16-p.17, p.55). Not chart.

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- "Unusually large volume" and "large volumes of transactions" have no number; the book only says volume is judged relative to each stock's normal activity (p.13).
- The allowance for a "second movement" to count as reaching the first extreme is stated only in 1898-1899 points (fractions; one to three points in wide stocks; five or six points in a stock that moved 30 points in two days) (p.11, p.44, p.47). Book is silent on any scaling rule.
- The chart he uses records only swings of one point or more (p.43, p.46). A modern candle chart shows more detail; S1-S9 are written for swings visible on such a chart, which is our reading, not his construction.
- He says a break above old tops "does not occur once out of five times" in one example (p.59) and also that prices "just as sure" break through old points at times (p.59, p.51). The book gives no rule for which case to expect except volume and the general market.
- The booklet is partly self-promotion; its forecast record and account results are self-reported (p.40-p.42, p.51, p.76).
