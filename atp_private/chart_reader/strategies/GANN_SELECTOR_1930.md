# GANN_SELECTOR_1930: Wall Street Stock Selector (W. D. Gann, 1930)

## 1. The book's idea in one paragraph
The 1930 companion to *Truth of the Stock Tape* keeps the same structure reading and states it as firmer rules (p.207, p.278). Old tops and bottoms from charts going back ten years or more are the Resistance Levels (p.278). Gann does not trade inside a sideways range; he waits until price crosses 3 points above the old top level or breaks 3 points under the old low level, and only then commits (p.299). A trend change is accepted only after the bottoms and tops change direction (p.274-275). At an extreme he looks for exhaustion: a rally (or retest) on smaller volume that fails to pass the extreme, followed by narrow, low-volume bars; he then acts as soon as price leaves the level of those narrow bars, with the stop beyond the extreme (p.275). Each stock is judged on its own swings, and in each group the strongest is bought and the weakest sold (p.241-242, p.256-257, p.341).

## 2. What the book reads on the market (trend, levels, volume, bar/tape) - with p.NN
- **Trend:** higher bottoms and higher tops = up; lower tops and lower bottoms = down; wait for one or two of them before accepting a change (p.274-275). A higher bottom that fails to make a higher top (does not cross the level from which the last decline started) shows buying power is short (p.275). Swing charts of major tops and bottoms show the main trend (p.322, p.326-327, p.331-337).
- **Levels:** old tops and old bottoms from a long chart (p.278). Repeated lows at one level = buying level (Davison Chemical 21-23, AT&T 88-91) (p.328, p.343-345). Repeated tops at one level = selling level (U.S. Steel 192-193, AT&T near 108) (p.301, p.328, p.334). Role reversal: old tops later gave support, and an old support later acted as resistance (U.S. Steel, Industrial Alcohol 95-98) (p.333-334, p.347). The point from which the last move started is watched on the way back (p.302).
- **Sideways movements:** periods of rest, accumulation or distribution that fool traders until price leaves the range (p.297-299).
- **Reactions:** the first reaction in a new move sets a measure; later reactions of about the same size are normal; a much larger reaction is a danger signal (p.278-279, p.389). Watch the second and third top or bottom; failure to reach the first is a warning (p.279).
- **Volume:** rises in advances, dries up when liquidation ends; compare the volume of a rally with the volume of the decline before it (p.286-292). Small volume on the final test of a low suggests bottom (p.291-292, p.393). "As soon as the volume begins to show a decrease, then look for a change in trend" (p.229).
- **Bar:** a bar that reaches a new extreme and closes at the opposite end of its range on heavy volume is a rejection (U.S. Steel, Industrial Alcohol at their tops) (p.291, p.348). A fast, near-vertical "final grand rush" up, or a panicky fast decline, marks the end of a campaign (p.295-297).

## 3. Setup conditions (numbered S1..Sn; each one observable on a price/volume chart; each with p.NN)
Long side shown; the book states the mirror for the short side (p.274-275, p.299).
- **S1 - Rising structure:** the swing lows and swing highs on the chart are rising; after a decline, at least one higher bottom and one higher top have formed (p.274-275).
- **S2 - Higher top confirmed:** the latest higher bottom has been followed by a rally that crossed the level from which the last decline started (not a higher bottom without a higher top) (p.275).
- **S3 - Old top level marked:** a horizontal level of old tops is visible where price stopped more than once over a long stretch of the chart (p.278, p.331-337).
- **S4 - Sideways range:** before the trigger, price has moved sideways for many bars between a flat upper edge and a flat lower edge (p.297-299).
- **S5 - Exhaustion at a low:** after a sharp decline on large volume and a quick rally, price comes back near the extreme low but does not break it, while bar ranges narrow and volume bars get smaller (p.275); a later test of the low on smaller volume than the first (p.291, p.393).
- **S6 - Volume comparison:** the volume bars on the latest advance are larger than those on the decline before it; or the decline into the low shows shrinking volume (p.286-292).
- **S7 - Normal reactions:** within the advance, the latest pullback is about the same size as, or smaller than, the first reaction of the move (not much larger) (p.278-279, p.389).
- **S8 - Old level holding from above:** after crossing an old top, price reacts back toward that level and holds near it (old top acting as support) (p.278, p.333-334, p.347).
- **S9 - Relative strength (optional, needs a second chart):** the stock is among the strongest of its group, holding higher while others in the group make lower levels (p.256-257, p.341, p.399-401).

## 4. Trigger (the book's own signal to act, as the book states it, even if it differs from Abhi's method) - with p.NN
- **A. Breakout from the sideways range / old top.** Leave the stock alone while it is in a sideways movement and wait until it crosses 3 points above the old top level, or breaks 3 points under the old low level, before making the commitment (Jewel Tea, 1922-1925) (p.299). When a stock crosses an old top made over months or years (Gann's example: 100), there is reason to expect higher prices "and you should buy it" (p.278).
- **B. Exhaustion turn at an extreme.** At a bottom: after a panicky decline and a quick rally, if price returns near the extreme low without breaking it while fluctuations narrow and volume shrinks, buy with a stop under the extreme, or buy as soon as price crosses the level of the narrow-range bars (p.275). Mirror at a top: rally on smaller volume that fails to reach the extreme high, narrow low-volume bars near the rally high, then sell short as soon as price breaks under the level of those narrow bars, stop just above the extreme high (p.275).
- In both cases Gann first wants the trend shown by the bottoms and tops: buy only after the bottoms and tops begin to rise, sell short only after one or two lower tops and lower bottoms (p.274-275).

## 5. Invalidation (what the book says proves the read wrong) - with p.NN
- After buying on the cross of an old top, price reacts back well below that top (Gann's example: bought on the cross of 100, reached 103; a reaction to 97 means it is not going up soon, "you had better be out of it") (p.278).
- The second or third top (or bottom) fails to go as high (or as low) as the first: "get out and wait" (p.279).
- A reaction much larger than the earlier reactions in the move (10 points where 5-7 was normal) is a danger signal; sell on the next rally (p.278-279, p.322).
- The stock cannot make a higher bottom and hold it: weak position, not a buy (p.274).
- A higher bottom fails to make a higher top (buying power short) (p.275).
- One or two lower tops and lower bottoms form after the advance (trend reversed) (p.274-275).
- A bar at a new high closes at its low on heavy volume (rejection) (p.291, p.348).

## 6. Book's own exits / stops / size (book text only; in ATP these are the user's dashboard settings, never defaults)
- Stops of 3-5 points; 3 points beyond old levels; 1 point beyond for low-priced stocks; a trailing stop equal to the last reaction; 10 points from the top for investors (p.230, p.278, p.302, p.322-323, p.343).
- After a cross of an old top: stop at the level 3 points under the crossed top (100 crossed, stop 97) (p.278); a 2-point stop after a cross in some cases (p.343).
- Exhaustion trade: stop just beyond the extreme high or low (p.275).
- Risk 1/10 of capital per trade; spread risk over 4-5 stocks; build a surplus (p.230-232).
- Move the stop to break even after a 3-point gain (4-5 points in high-priced stocks) (p.232).
- Do not risk 3-5 points to make 3-5 points; use a 1-2 point stop in that case (p.236).
- Pyramiding every 3, 5 or 10 points, or after measured reactions; small-float stocks for longs, large-float stocks for shorts (p.231, p.235-236).
- Take profits on days of a fast advance or a panicky decline; it is better to get out too soon than too late (p.261, p.279).
- No averaging of losses (p.230-240).

## 7. Not coded: time-based rules (C.4) and anything not visible on a chart (news, tips, fundamentals)
- Cycles of panics and history repeating by years; an 8-year bull market as in 1861-69 (p.215-224).
- A man's own trend/cycle: quit after two or three losses (p.246-249).
- Time charts "20 days, 20 weeks, 20 months, 20 years" (p.257-260).
- 1st-4th stage counting: culmination on the 3rd or 4th move (p.260-261).
- Daily time rule: wait for a 2-3 day halt, then trade with a 3-point stop (p.261).
- Weekly time rule: 2-3 week reactions, the 3rd week, culmination in the 6th-7th week (p.262).
- Monthly time rule: no reaction into a 2nd month; watch the 3rd-4th month (p.262-263).
- Days of the week: Monday first hour, Wednesday afternoon, Thursday morning, Friday (p.263-264).
- Dates in the month: 1st-3rd, 10th, 15th, 20th-23rd; U.S. Steel monthly turning dates (p.264-267).
- Months to watch: every 3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th month and one-year anniversaries of tops and bottoms (p.267).
- Favourite months per stock (U.S. Steel, General Motors) (p.267-271).
- Pyramiding after each 2-3 week reaction (General Motors) (p.235).
- Fast-move time limits: final grand rush of 6-7 (up to 10) weeks; fast moves of 3-10 days (p.295-296, p.305).
- Time limit to hold a stock: about 3 weeks; investment stocks 2-3 months (p.307).
- "Rule of Three" and "balancing" a stock: count of consecutive higher/lower closes; three or more closes near one level (p.279-284).
- Holding a higher bottom "for a few days, or a week" (the time part of p.274).
- 1929 Annual Forecast and its "permanent cycle" dates (p.445-480 approx., dates p.458-459).
- "Master Time Factor", vibration, wave length, about-20-year panic and "volcanic eruption" cycles (p.309, p.414-416, p.473-474 approx.).
- Volume-screen "new way to read the tape": trade a stock when its daily volume jumps from under 10,000 to 25,000 shares (absolute share counts across stocks; a screen, not a chart read) (p.253-254).
- Round-number levels and order placement 1/8-1/4 inside even figures (not derived from chart structure) (p.227, p.275-277, p.299-302).
- Buying 3 points over the high of "the first year of the bull campaign" (a calendar-year level) (p.342-343, p.391).
- Money rates, bond prices, control by Morgan/du Pont, splits, dividends, investment trusts, mergers, war (p.316-320, p.410-416).
- Stock-picking lists, paid forecast service and course adverts (p.403-409, p.433-488 approx.).

## 9. Open points / limits of the book
- The 3-point breakout filter (p.299) and the 3-point stop below a crossed top (p.278) are absolute distances in 1920s US stock points; the book gives no rule for scaling them to other price levels or instruments. In this spec the 3-point figure is part of the book's own entry signal and is reported as the book states it; it is not a default.
- "Sharp", "large volume", "narrow", "many bars" have no numeric thresholds in the book.
- The exhaustion rule (p.275) mixes a structure read with "a few days of dullness"; only the structure part (narrow, low-volume bars, no new extreme, price leaving their level) is coded; the day count is listed in section 7.
- The book overlaps heavily with *Truth of the Stock Tape* (Gann refers back to it, p.278); the two specs are kept separate as requested, and this one uses only the 1930 text.
- OCR numbers are often damaged; the U.S. Steel balance tables and appendix price tables are unreadable (see note section 8). Charts were not read as images.
